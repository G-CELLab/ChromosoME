# Mitosis Basics (Tutor Version)

## Core Goal
Mitosis makes two genetically identical daughter cells.
It supports growth and tissue repair.

## Big Picture: DNA and Chromosomes
- DNA is stored in the nucleus.
- DNA wraps around proteins and coils to form chromosomes.
- DNA is not always tightly packed; it condenses into visible chromosomes when the cell is preparing to divide.
- If DNA stayed as loose strands during division, accurate separation would be much harder.

## Replication Before Mitosis
- DNA is copied during S phase of the cell cycle, before mitosis.
- The copied DNA allows formation of two equivalent nuclei.
- After mitosis and cytokinesis, each daughter cell receives a complete set of genetic instructions.

## Chromosome Structure Essentials
- A replicated chromosome has two identical sister chromatids.
- The sister chromatids are joined at the centromere.
- Spindle microtubules attach at/near the centromere (kinetochore region).
- During anaphase, sister chromatids separate and each chromatid is then considered an individual chromosome.
- In humans, cells typically have 23 pairs of chromosomes.

## Phase Order
Interphase -> Prophase -> Metaphase -> Anaphase -> Telophase

## Interphase
- The cell grows, checks readiness, and prepares for division.
- In this project context, mitochondria use nutrients to generate energy needed for upcoming steps.
- DNA replication has already occurred by the end of S phase.

Short answer:
Interphase is the preparation stage: the cell builds resources, replicates DNA (in S phase), and gets ready for mitosis.

## Prophase
- DNA condenses into visible chromosomes.
- The nuclear envelope begins to break down.
- Spindle structures begin forming and start attaching to chromosomes.

Short answer:
In prophase, DNA condenses into chromosomes and the cell builds the spindle system for chromosome movement.

## Metaphase
- Chromosomes align at the center of the cell (metaphase plate/equator).
- Spindle fibers from opposite poles attach so chromosomes can be separated accurately.

Short answer:
In metaphase, chromosomes line up in the middle for equal distribution to both future cells.

## Anaphase
- Sister chromatids separate.
- Spindle fibers pull chromatids to opposite poles.
- At this point, each separated chromatid is now an individual chromosome.

Short answer:
In anaphase, sister chromatids split and move quickly to opposite sides of the cell.

## Telophase
- Chromosomes arrive at opposite poles.
- Nuclear envelopes reform around each chromosome set.
- Cytoplasm divides and the parent cell physically separates.
- Two genetically identical daughter cells are produced.

Short answer:
In telophase, two new nuclei form at opposite ends, and cytokinesis occurs to divide the cytoplasm.

## Vocabulary
- Chromosome: condensed DNA carrying genes.
- Chromatid: one half of a duplicated chromosome.
- Sister chromatids: matching duplicated chromatids joined before separation.
- Centromere: connection point between sister chromatids.
- Spindle fibers: microtubule structures that move chromosomes.
- Nuclear envelope: membrane surrounding the nucleus.
- Metaphase plate: center plane where chromosomes align.

## Cross-Phase Rule
If the learner asks about another phase, answer that phase directly and correctly, even if the current scene phase is different.

## Common Clarifications for Learners
- Why do chromosomes line up in metaphase? To ensure each daughter cell gets one complete copy of every chromosome.
- When do nuclear envelopes reform? During telophase.
- Which stage do many cells spend most of their time in? Interphase.
- Why is anaphase often short? Once spindle forces are established, chromatid separation is rapid.
- What is the classic X shape? A duplicated chromosome made of two sister chromatids.

## Mitosis vs Meiosis Note (For Comparison Questions)
- In mitosis metaphase, individual chromosomes align at the metaphase plate.
- In meiosis I metaphase, homologous chromosome pairs align.
- Prophase I of meiosis includes homolog pairing and crossing-over, which does not occur in mitosis prophase.

## Short Tutor Responses (Examples)
- "What is mitosis?" -> "Mitosis is cell division that makes two genetically identical daughter cells."
- "What is DNA made of in this context?" -> "DNA is packed around proteins to form chromosomes during division."
- "Why do chromosomes line up in the middle?" -> "So each new cell gets a complete, matching set of DNA."
- "What happens in anaphase?" -> "Sister chromatids split and move to opposite poles."
- "What should I do in prophase?" -> "Hold the red DNA steady so it condenses into the X-shaped chromosome."
